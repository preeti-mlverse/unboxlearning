From your material to a course, in five steps.

You stay in charge of what gets taught. The engine does the reading, the planning and the writing, and shows its work at every step.

1

Add your material

Upload files or paste a link. Mix several sources into one course: a textbook chapter, a recorded lecture and your own notes can sit side by side.

The engine keeps the structure of what you give it, including headings, tables, code, figures and slide notes, so nothing important is flattened into plain text.

2

The engine reads it like a teacher

It works out what kind of material this is, what subject it covers and what kinds of knowledge it holds: facts to remember, processes to follow, causes and effects, procedures to carry out.

That mix decides how it will be taught. A process gets step diagrams and put-it-in-order games. Vocabulary gets match-ups and a memory deck. A policy gets decision scenarios.

3

You set the goal

Pick one of the goals the engine suggests or write your own. Say who the learners are, how much time they have and which language they learn in.

Sections that aren't worth teaching, such as contents pages, reference lists and legal boilerplate, are left out by default. You can tick them back in.

4

You approve the outline

Before a single card is written you see the journey: short modules, each with one clear goal. Rename, reorder, add or remove modules, then press Generate.

The engine also tells you what it left out on purpose, and why.

5

Cards, games and checks

Each module becomes about a dozen cards: a hook, picture-first explanations, at least three quick games, a hands-on task and a recap. Narration and flashcards are made at the same time.

Every card is checked for length and reading level, every game for a working answer key, and every fact against your source. Anything doubtful is flagged for you to fix before learners see it.

Questions

How it works, in detail.

How long does it take to make a course?

Reading and understanding your material takes about a minute. The outline takes one or two more. Generating a 20-minute course takes two to five minutes, depending on how many modules and pictures it has.

Can I edit what the engine writes?

Yes. You can edit any card yourself, ask the AI to rewrite it with an instruction such as "simpler words" or "use an Indian example", or ask for a new picture.

Does it make things up?

Cards are written only from the passages of your material that support them, then checked against those passages by a second pass. Anything that isn't supported is flagged for you to review.

Which languages does it support?

Courses can be made in English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Spanish, French, German and Arabic, and learners can switch language without losing progress.

Who owns the courses?

You do. Your material and the courses made from it are yours. We don't use your content to train AI models.
